Core conceptual objections (short)
Piezoelectricity needs piezoelectric material and net alignment/poling of domains/crystals so stress produces a macroscopic polarization. Natural building stone blocks are aggregate, polycrystalline, unoriented, and unpoled — their microscopic contributions cancel to near zero.
Real piezoelectric transducers use electrodes, controlled poling, and materials (quartz, PZT) chosen and shaped for that purpose. The pyramid has none of those.
Static compressive stress gives bound charge that is rapidly screened by finite conductivity, moisture, and contact conduction. A usable transducer needs dynamic strain at the device’s resonance, low leakage, and engineered coupling.
Mechanical damping and the pyramid’s construction (massive masonry, joints, rubble infill) produce very low Q and prevent 
efficient resonant conversion of mechanical energy to electrical energy on a building scale.

Now a best‑case piezoelectric estimate

Use a favorable single-crystal piezoelectric coefficient as a benchmark: quartz d ≈ 2.3 pC/N = 2.3×10^-12 C/N. (Commercial piezo ceramics can be orders of magnitude larger, but those are engineered and poled; natural rock is far worse.)
Surface charge density produced by stress: D = d · σ. Using σ ≈ 1×10^6 Pa and d = 2.3×10^-12 C/N gives D ≈ 2.3×10^-6 C/m^2.
For a 1 m thickness of material with relative permittivity εr ≈ 5, ε ≈ 5ε0 ≈ 4.4×10^-11 F/m. The open‑circuit voltage across 1 m of that material would be V ≈ D·t/ε ≈ 2.3×10^-6 / 4.4×10^-11 ≈ 5.2×10^4 V (≈ 52 kV). That looks large but it is misleading because:
It assumes the entire meter‑thick slab is a single, uniformly poled piezoelectric crystal (it is not).
It ignores screening and leakage (see below).
It ignores that the piezoelectric coefficient used is for a well-oriented crystal; random orientation in rock reduces the effective d by many orders of magnitude (roughly as 1/√N for N randomly oriented grains).
Charge per m^2: Q = D·A1 ≈ 2.3×10^-6 C for A1 = 1 m^2. If that charge were collected over 1 s, the current would be ~2.3 μA per m^2 — tiny.
Even if you multiply by the whole base area (unrealistic full coherence), Q_total ≈ D·A ≈ 0.12 C and if discharged in 1 s that’s 0.12 A. But coherent summation over millions is impossible because grains are randomly oriented and internal charges cancel; you would need the stones poled and electrically connected like electrodes to add coherently.

Leakage and screening kill any static effect

A (very conservative) estimate of dielectric permittivity ε ≈ 4.4×10^-11 F/m and a realistic conductivity σ for stone (with moisture and surface conduction) is not vanishingly small — values of σ in rocks and moisture‑containing masonry are commonly in the 10^-9 — 10^-4 S/m range depending on humidity. The dielectric relaxation time τ ≈ ε/σ:
If σ = 10^-6 S/m (moderately moist), τ ≈ 4.4×10^-5 s.
Even for very dry stone σ = 10^-9 S/m, τ ≈ 4.4×10^-2 s.
In short: any bound charge generated by a static stress dissipates on millisecond–centisecond timescales. The pyramid is not a high‑Q capacitor that can hold charge for hours.
Practical consequence: static compressive stress cannot produce a steady high voltage that you can harvest; any generated charge is neutralized almost immediately by conduction through contacts, humidity films, and air leakage.

Coherence and orientation

Building stones are polycrystalline aggregates. Piezoelectricity from individual mineral grains (e.g., trace quartz) is vectorial; without poling the vector sum over an astronomically large number of randomly oriented grains is essentially zero. You would need engineered poling or single-crystal continuity across many meters which the pyramid does not have.

Mechanical drive and resonance problems

To continuously convert mechanical energy to electric energy you need dynamic strain and reasonably high Q resonance or continuous oscillation. The pyramid’s construction (gaps, mortar, rubble cores) is strongly damped; mechanical resonances are broad and weakly coupled to any microscopic piezoelectric constituents.
Exciting the whole structure at a mechanical resonance with sufficient amplitude requires enormous mechanical energy input (far larger than plausible natural sources) and still would not produce coherent electrical output because of the orientation/leakage problems.

Bottom-line numerical intuition

Even taking wildly optimistic assumptions (uniform, aligned piezoelectric stone with quartz‑like d and no leakage), you get tiny currents unless you unphysically assume perfect coherence over the whole structure. Real materials, random orientation, contacts, and leakage reduce any electrical signal by many orders of magnitude. The result: effectively zero usable power or signal.
Direct experiments or measurements that decisively disprove the page. These are simple, reproducible, and inexpensive measurements.

A. On an individual stone block (controlled)

Take a typical pyramid block (or a laboratory sample of limestone/granite from the site).
Put electrodes on opposite faces (conductive paint or foil), connect to a charge amplifier / electrometer (input impedance >10^13–10^14 Ω) and a data logger.
Apply a known cyclic stress/strain (mechanical press, controlled vibration) with amplitude comparable to the block’s in‑situ stress changes and frequency sweep across 0.1–1000 Hz.
Measure charge, voltage, and current. Expected result: below instrument noise or vanishingly small signals consistent with known tiny mineral piezoelectric coefficients and screening.

B. On the structure (in situ, non‑destructive)

Place high‑impedance voltage probes between the apex and a base electrode (use conductive pads to make contact).
Instrument mechanical exciters (shakers) or use controlled impacts at known locations while recording electrical and accelerometer signals simultaneously.
If the pyramid were a transducer, you would see correlated electrical signals with the mechanical driving that scale with drive amplitude and show narrowband resonance — you will not.

C. Measure dielectric conductivity/leakage (simple)

Measure DC conductivity between two electrodes on a block (and use small AC to get permittivity).
From σ and ε compute τ = ε/σ. Expect τ << 1 s in real conditions — showing any piezoelectric charge is rapidly lost.

D. If someone claims prior measurements that show large signals

Insist on raw data, time stamps, instrument calibration, control measurements, electrode geometry, humidity/temperature logs, and replication by an independent lab. Extraordinary claims require extraordinary reproducibility.
Why reports of “electromagnetic effects from stones” exist but don’t support the claim
Stressed rocks can emit tiny electromagnetic transients in certain conditions (microfracturing, triboelectric effects, stress‑activated defect migration). These are local, weak, and inconsistent with an engineered piezoelectric transducer claim.
Such phenomena are small, transient, and require freshly stressed/fracturing rock or specific mineralogy; they are not evidence the pyramid is a coherent, large‑scale energy transducer.
